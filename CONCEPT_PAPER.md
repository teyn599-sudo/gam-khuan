## 1. Executive Summary

Project Gam-Khuan proposes a dual-core planetary engineering system that integrates:

- **Core 1:** Transfer of seawater from the ocean into characterized coastal desert geological formations for long-term isolation, contributing to net ocean mass reduction.
- **Core 2:** Evolution of the extracted brine into a solid-state salt-silica-carbon crystalline matrix serving as a non-metallic, grid-scale energy storage medium.

Both cores share the same infrastructure: conveyance, basin, and carbon network. Water transfer provides the material for the solid-state capacitor; the capacitor provides the energy storage that makes the system economically self-sustaining.

## 2. Problem Statement

### 2.1 Sea-Level Rise

Global mean sea level is rising. Coastal habitability is threatened. Current mitigation strategies (emission reduction, coastal defenses) address the cause or the symptom but do not remove water from the ocean.

### 2.2 Grid-Scale Energy Storage

The transition to renewable energy requires massive, low-cost, long-duration energy storage. Current lithium-ion and flow-battery solutions are constrained by material cost, supply chain risk, and geographic limitations.

### 2.3 The Opportunity

A coastal desert basin — characterized by low elevation, high evaporation, geological isolation, and proximity to the ocean — is the minimum viable unit for addressing both problems simultaneously.

## 3. Technical Architecture

### 3.1 Module 1 — Conveyance

Gravity-fed pressurized tunnels or channels convey seawater from the ocean to the inland desert basin. Natural elevation differences minimize external energy input.

### 3.2 Module 2 — Geological Sequestration

Seawater is directed into deep permeable layers, saline aquifers, and mineral-rich formations. Chambers are situated within ultralow-permeability host rock (Lugeon value < 0.01). Site characterization requires 3D reflection seismic and borehole packer testing prior to excavation.

### 3.3 Module 3 — Solid-State Energy Storage

Two operational phases:

**Phase 1 (Liquid, TRL 4-5):** Immediate deployment using raw seawater/brine for double-layer capacitor storage while evaporation progresses.

- Energy density: > 5 Wh/L
- Cycle life: > 10,000 cycles
- Efficiency: > 70%

**Phase 2 (Solid, TRL 2-3):** Terminal solid-state crystallization into a salt-silica-carbon nano-composite dielectric material.

- Effective dielectric constant: > 100 initial, > 1000 scaled
- Breakdown field strength: > 50 MV/m
- Energy density: > 10 Wh/L initial, > 50 Wh/L scaled
- Leakage current: < 1% per month
- Cycle life: > 100,000 cycles

### 3.4 Module 4 — Atmospheric Electrostatic Bias

Laser-induced plasma channels and tree-branching carbon-fiber lightning masts provide atmospheric electrostatic bias to the salt-silica-carbon matrix. This is an integrated bias subsystem, not a baseload generation source.

## 4. Mass-Balance Framework

Net Ocean Water Removal is defined as:

    V_net(t) = V_transferred(t) - V_evaporated(t) - V_returned(t) - V_leaked(t)

Retention fraction:

    R(t) = V_net(t) / V_transferred(t)

Target: R(100 years) > 80%.

Sea-level response requires modeling of effective ocean area, seawater density, gravitational fingerprints, and crustal response.
